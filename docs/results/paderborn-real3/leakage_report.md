# Leakage experiment: paderborn-kat

> How much does a random segment split overstate the group-split score?

## Setup

- Rows: 4765 (dropped for non-finite features: 0); groups per class: {"healthy": 5, "inner": 5, "outer": 5}
- 5-fold, 10 repeats (fold seeds 0..9); chance balanced accuracy 0.333
- Features: `rms`, `kurtosis`, `crest_factor`, `impulse_factor`, `spectral_centroid_hz`, `hf_energy_ratio`, `env_bpfo_ratio`, `env_bpfi_ratio`

## Results (balanced accuracy, mean over repeats)

| Model | Group k-fold | Random k-fold | Inflation mean [min, max] | Repeats inflated |

| nearest_centroid | 0.575 | 0.620 | +0.044 [+0.019, +0.065] | 10 of 10 |

## How to read this

- Only the **group k-fold** column estimates performance on units the model has not seen. Never report the random-split column as a result.
- Inflation that is positive in most repeats means segments of one group share information unrelated to the label (unit identity, mounting, an individual defect) and the model uses it. Flexible models (random forest) usually exploit it more than simple ones.
- Small or negative inflation does not prove the absence of leakage in other data. It only says this table, with these features, shows no gap.
